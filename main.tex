\section{Introduction}

Optimal transport (OT) provides a geometric framework for comparing distributions and aligning datasets, while unbalanced optimal transport (UOT) relaxes marginal constraints to allow mass variation~\citep{chizat2018scaling,sejourne2023unbalanced}.
% CN: 先介绍单细胞应用，再列举其他领域及其需要边际放松的原因。
% EN: Introduce single-cell applications first, followed by other fields and their motivations for marginal relaxation.
This flexibility supports applications in single-cell genomics~\citep{schiebinger2019optimal,klein2025mapping}, domain adaptation~\citep{fatras2021unbalanced}, and point-cloud motion estimation~\citep{puy2020flot}, accommodating cell proliferation and death, mismatched class proportions, and occlusions.

Let $\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb R_{++}^m$ and $\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb R_{++}^n$ be the reference masses of two discrete measures, and let $\mathbf{C}\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}$ be their pairwise cost matrix.
UOT minimizes transport cost plus divergence penalties on the deviations of the transported marginals from $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$.
% \xwz{The general-marginal-penalty appendix is temporarily omitted; restore its cross-reference only after that extension is written.}
We focus on KL marginal penalties:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:primal-uot}
\min_{\mathbf{X}\geq 0}\quad \langle \mathbf{C},\mathbf{X}\rangle+\rho_a\KL(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{1}_n\,|\,\mathbf{a})
+\rho_b\KL(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{1}_m\,|\,\mathbf{b}),
\end{equation}
Here, $\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb R_+^{m\times n}$ is the transport plan, $\rho_a,\rho_b>0$ control the marginal penalties, and $\KL(\mathbf{p}\,|\,\mathbf{h})=\sum_k(p_k\log(p_k/\mathbf{h}_k)-p_k+\mathbf{h}_k)$.
Appendix~\ref{app:l2-penalty} extends the method and convergence guarantees to squared-$\ell_2$ marginal penalties.

The dual of~\eqref{eq:primal-uot} optimizes potentials $\mathbf{f}\in\mathbb R^m$ and $\mathbf{g}\in\mathbb R^n$:
\begin{equation} 
\label{eq:dual-uot}
\begin{aligned}
&D(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}):=\rho_a\langle \mathbf{a},\mathbf{1}_m-e^{-\mathbf{f}/\rho_a}\rangle
+\rho_b\langle \mathbf{b},\mathbf{1}_n-e^{-\mathbf{g}/\rho_b}\rangle,\\
&\max_{\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}}D(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})\quad\text{s.t.}\quad f_i+g_j\leq C_{ij}\quad\forall i,j.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The primal formulation has one transport variable for each of the $mn$ source--target pairs; the dual has only $m+n$ potentials but still imposes $mn$ constraints.
This quadratic problem size poses substantial memory and computational challenges at large sample sizes.

\paragraph{Related work.}
Regularization methods add penalties to the transport plan to facilitate optimization, including squared-$\ell_2$ regularization~\citep{nguyen2023unbalanced} and, most prominently, entropic regularization.
The latter enables generalized \sinkhorn iterations, including stabilized and translation-invariant scaling variants~\citep{chizat2018scaling,schmitzer2019stabilized,pham2020unbalanced,sejourne2022faster}.
However, additional regularization introduces bias relative to unregularized UOT, and entropic regularization generally yields a dense coupling whose explicit materialization requires $\Theta(mn)$ memory.

Low-rank methods constrain the coupling to admit a compact nonnegative factorization, improving computational and memory scalability at a fixed rank~\citep{scetbon2023unbalanced,halmos2024low}.
This scalability comes at the cost of potentially substantial approximation error when the chosen rank is insufficient to represent the optimal coupling.

Several methods target the original UOT objective directly, including multiplicative coupling updates (\mm)~\citep{chapel2021unbalanced}, marginal-first reformulations (\etm)~\citep{liu2026solving}, and Frank--Wolfe reductions such as \hellouot~\citep{sejourne2022faster,xia2026dual}.
However, standard \mm implementations store the full coupling; \etm requires Sinkhorn-type $O(mn)$ updates for marginal estimation; and \hellouot repeatedly invokes balanced OT oracles.
% Another approach exploits coupling sparsity through a network-simplex framework that maintains forest-supported transport plans~\citep{agazzotti2026network}.
% Scalability remains limited by quadratic coupling storage in standard \mm implementations, the cost of marginal estimation or sequential balanced OT subproblems in reduction methods, and sequential tree updates in network simplex that hinder GPU parallelization.
% This leaves open how to solve the large scale unregularized UOT problem with linear memory and efficient GPU parallelism.

Existing methods that exploit coupling sparsity face different limitations: regularization paths require quadratic marginal penalties~\citep{chapel2021unbalanced}; network simplex relies on sequential tree updates that complicate GPU parallelization~\citep{agazzotti2026network}; and safe screening provides no linear-memory guarantee throughout optimization~\citep{su2025safe}.
This leaves open how to solve large-scale UOT problems with linear memory and efficient GPU parallelism.

\paragraph{Our approach.}
UOT admits sparse optimal couplings, motivating optimization over a small active support.
We introduce \ours, a \textbf{S}calable \textbf{A}ctive-support solver for \textbf{L}arge-scale \textbf{U}nbalanced optimal \textbf{T}ransport.
It alternates optimization on the active support with support updates to identify missing transport variables.

A central computational bottleneck is the repeated solution of nonlinear restricted UOT subproblems.
To address this bottleneck, we develop \subsolver, a GPU-efficient first-order primal--dual solver that returns both the coupling and dual potentials for subsequent support updates.
Following \hello's dual-guided hierarchy~\citep{xia2026dual}, we use dual potentials from smaller problems to initialize the active support and dual violations to guide support updates.
To guarantee linear working memory, we combine budgeted pruning with support sparsification that preserves both transported marginals and transport cost.

Our contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Scalable active-support optimization:} We introduce \ours, which solves large-scale UOT through restricted optimization and dynamic support refinement using linear memory.
Experiments demonstrate million-point scalability and applicability across cost and marginal penalty types.
\item \textit{GPU-efficient restricted solver:} For repeated restricted subproblem solves, we develop \subsolver, a GPU-friendly first-order solver that efficiently provides high-accuracy primal--dual solutions across a wide range of marginal penalty strengths. 
   \item \textit{Convergence guarantees:} We establish global convergence and a local linear rate for the full \subsolver algorithm.
    For \ours, we prove finite termination at a prescribed full-space KKT tolerance with inexact restricted solves under the implemented stopping rules.
    \item \textit{Extensions and applications:} We extend \ours to approximate entropic UOT and solve unbalanced fused Gromov--Wasserstein (UFGW) problems, with large-scale biological experiments showing substantial speedups and competitive downstream performance.
\end{itemize}

\section{Active-Support Optimization for UOT}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overall Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

With the transported marginals $\mathbf{X}\mathbf{1}_n$ and $\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{1}_m$ fixed, the marginal penalties are constant and minimizing over $\mathbf{X}$ reduces to balanced OT.
Consequently, whenever an optimum exists, there is an optimal coupling with at most $m+n-1$ positive entries.
On an active support $\mathcal N\subseteq[m]\times[n]$, the restricted primal--dual pair is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:restricted-uot}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\mathbf X\geq0}\Phi(\mathbf X)\quad&\text{s.t.}\quad \supp(\mathbf X)\subseteq\mathcal N,\\
\max_{\mathbf f,\mathbf g}D(\mathbf f,\mathbf g)
\quad&\text{s.t.}\quad f_i+g_j\leq C_{ij},\quad(i,j)\in\mathcal N,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\Phi$ and $D$ are the objectives in~\eqref{eq:primal-uot} and~\eqref{eq:dual-uot}, respectively.

The potentials returned by a restricted solve define the dual score
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dual-score}
\sigma_{ij}:=f_i+g_j-C_{ij}.
\end{equation}
A positive omitted score identifies a violated dual constraint and provides a candidate for support expansion.
If every omitted score is nonpositive at an exact restricted primal--dual solution, that solution is globally optimal for the full problem (Proposition~\ref{prop:restricted-recovery}, Appendix~\ref{app:refinement-convergence}).
Inspired by column generation for linear programming~\citep{lubbecke2005selected}, we alternate between solving restricted UOT subproblems and updating the active support using dual scores.
The algorithm stops when the full-space KKT tolerance is met; Section~\ref{sec:analysis} establishes finite termination under the stated conditions with inexact restricted solves.

Repeated nonlinear restricted UOT solves are a core computational bottleneck of this approach.
We develop \subsolver to efficiently obtain high-accuracy primal--dual solutions across marginal penalty strengths (Section~\ref{sec:pduot}), with the returned potentials also guiding subsequent support updates.

A good initial active support can reduce subsequent work~\citep{schmitzer2016sparse,xia2026memory}, so we construct a coarse-to-fine hierarchy of UOT problems and use smaller-problem solutions for initialization, following \hello~\citep{xia2026dual}.
Budgeted pruning and support sparsification as needed control support size to maintain linear working memory (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}).
Algorithm~\ref{alg:ours} summarizes the resulting method, with levels indexed from $0$, the finest, to $L$, the coarsest, and primal--dual states $\mathcal Z^{(\ell)}=(\mathbf X^{(\ell)},\mathbf f^{(\ell)},\mathbf g^{(\ell)})$.


\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\ours for UOT}
\label{alg:ours}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} UOT hierarchy $\{\mathcal P^{(\ell)}\}_{\ell=0}^{L}$, support budgets $\kappa,\gamma,\beta$, and tolerance $\varepsilon$.
\STATE \textbf{Output:} finest-level primal--dual state $\mathcal Z^{(0)}$.
\medskip
\STATE $\mathcal Z^{(L)}\gets\subsolver(\mathcal P^{(L)},[m_L]\times[n_L],\varnothing;\varepsilon)$
\FOR{$\ell=L-1,\ldots,0$}
    \STATE $\mathcal B\gets\nw(\mathcal P^{(\ell)})$
    \STATE $(\mathcal N,\mathcal Z)\gets\init(\mathcal P^{(\ell)},\mathcal Z^{(\ell+1)},\mathcal B;\kappa)$
    \LOOP
        \STATE $\mathcal Z\gets\subsolver(\mathcal P^{(\ell)},\mathcal N,\mathcal Z;\varepsilon)$
        \STATE $\mathcal Z\gets\sparsify(\mathcal Z)$
        \STATE \textbf{if} $\kkt(\mathcal P^{(\ell)},\mathcal Z)\leq\varepsilon$ \textbf{then break}
        \STATE $\mathcal V\gets\pricing(\mathcal P^{(\ell)},\mathcal Z;\gamma)$
        \STATE $\mathcal N\gets\prune(\mathcal N\cup\mathcal V,\mathcal Z,\mathcal B,\mathcal V;\beta)$
    \ENDLOOP
    \STATE $\mathcal Z^{(\ell)}\gets\mathcal Z$
\ENDFOR
\medskip
\STATE \textbf{Return:} $\mathcal Z^{(0)}$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Restricted UOT with \subsolver}
\label{sec:pduot}

For an active support $\mathcal N$, let $\mathbf x\in\mathbb R_+^{|\mathcal N|}$ collect its edge flows and $\mathbf c_{\mathcal N}\in\mathbb R^{|\mathcal N|}$ their costs.
The marginal-sum operator $\mathbf A_{\mathcal N}\in\mathbb R^{(m+n)\times|\mathcal N|}$ satisfies $\mathbf A_{\mathcal N}\mathbf x=(\mathbf X\mathbf1_n,\mathbf X^\top\mathbf1_m)$, where $\mathbf X\in\mathbb R_+^{m\times n}$ is zero outside $\mathcal N$.
With $G(\mathbf r,\mathbf q)=\rho_a\KL(\mathbf r\,|\,\mathbf a)+\rho_b\KL(\mathbf q\,|\,\mathbf b)$, problem~\eqref{eq:restricted-uot} becomes

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:restricted-uot-vector}
\min_{\mathbf{x}\geq0}\;\langle \mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{N}},\mathbf{x}\rangle+G(\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal N}\mathbf{x}).
\end{equation}

We build \subsolver on primal--dual hybrid gradient (PDHG) updates~\citep{esser2010general,chambolle2011first}.
Writing $F(\mathbf x)=\langle\mathbf c_{\mathcal N},\mathbf x\rangle+\iota_{\mathbb R_+^{|\mathcal N|}}(\mathbf x)$ and $\mathbf y=-(\mathbf f,\mathbf g)$ gives the saddle formulation
\begin{equation*}
\min_{\mathbf{x}}\max_{\mathbf{y}}\;F(\mathbf{x})+\langle \mathbf{A}_{\mathcal N}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle-G^*(\mathbf{y}),
\end{equation*}
where
\begin{equation*}
G^*(\mathbf{y})=\rho_a\sum_i a_i(e^{y_i/\rho_a}-1)+\rho_b\sum_j b_j(e^{y_{m+j}/\rho_b}-1).
\end{equation*}

With positive diagonal steps $\mathbf D_x,\mathbf D_y$, the primal-first PDHG map is $\pduotmap(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)=(\widehat{\mathbf x},\widehat{\mathbf y})$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pduot-map}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{\mathbf{x}}&=\left[\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{D}_x(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{N}}+\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal N}^\top \mathbf{y})\right]_+,\\
\widehat{\mathbf{y}}&=\operatorname{prox}_{G^*}^{\mathbf{D}_y^{-1}}\left(\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{D}_y\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal N}(2\widehat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x})\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation} 
The edgewise primal projection and nodewise dual proximal map enable sparse GPU-parallel updates.
Each node proximal is evaluated independently and stably (see Appendix~\ref{app:pduot-details}).

\paragraph{Reflected Halpern iteration.}
Following \citet{lu2024restarted,lin2025practical}, we use the reflected Halpern update
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf z^{k+1}
=\frac{\mathbf z^{\mathrm a}+(k+1)(2T\mathbf z^k-\mathbf z^k)}{k+2},
\end{equation*}
where $\mathbf z=(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)$, $\mathbf z^{\mathrm a}$ is the fixed epoch anchor, and $k$ counts iterations within the epoch.

\paragraph{Adaptive restart.}
Following \citet{lu2025cupdlpx}, we trigger a restart when the fixed-point residual decreases sufficiently from the epoch baseline, increases after moderate reduction, or the epoch exceeds a prescribed length.
See Appendix~\ref{app:pduot-controls} for the detailed criteria. 

\paragraph{Preconditioning.}
We use Pock--Chambolle diagonal preconditioning~\citep{pock2011diagonal} to set the base steps $\overline{\mathbf D}_x,\overline{\mathbf D}_y$.

\paragraph{Adaptive weight.}
A weight $w>0$ balances the primal and dual steps through $\mathbf D_x=w\overline{\mathbf D}_x$ and $\mathbf D_y=\overline{\mathbf D}_y/w$.
At restarts, we adapt $w$ using PID-based weight updates following \citet{lu2025cupdlpx}.
To establish convergence with adaptive weights, we additionally impose a summable budget on log-weight changes (see Appendix~\ref{app:pduot-controls}).

While the convergence theory underlying previous practical primal--dual solvers does not fully cover their practical combination of adaptive restarts and weight updates~\citep{lu2025cupdlpx,lin2025practical}, \subsolver admits convergence guarantees covering all four techniques described above, with the summable weight-update budget (Proposition~\ref{prop:pduot-convergence}).
Algorithm~\ref{alg:pduot} gives the complete iteration.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\subsolver for restricted UOT}
\label{alg:pduot}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\let\COMMENT\paperalgorithmcomment
\let\algorithmiccomment\paperalgorithmrightcomment
\STATE \textbf{Input:} problem $\mathcal P$, support $\mathcal N$, state $\mathbf z^0$, weight $w_0>0$, tolerance $\varepsilon$.
\STATE \textbf{Output:} coupling $\widehat{\mathbf x}$ and potentials $(\widehat{\mathbf f},\widehat{\mathbf g})$.
\medskip
\STATE $(\overline{\mathbf D}_x,\overline{\mathbf D}_y)\gets\precond(\mathbf A_{\mathcal N})$ \COMMENT{Preconditioning}
\STATE $\mathbf z^{\mathrm a}\gets\mathbf z^0$, $w\gets w_0$, $j\gets0$, $\mathcal H\gets\varnothing$
\LOOP
    \STATE $\mathbf D_x\gets w\overline{\mathbf D}_x$, $\mathbf D_y\gets\overline{\mathbf D}_y/w$
    \STATE $\mathbf z\gets\mathbf z^{\mathrm a}$, $k\gets0$
    \LOOP
        \STATE $(\widehat{\mathbf z},\boldsymbol\lambda,r)\gets\pdhg(\mathbf z;\mathbf D_x,\mathbf D_y)$
        \STATE \textbf{if} $\checkstop(\widehat{\mathbf z},\boldsymbol\lambda;\varepsilon)$ \textbf{then return} $(\widehat{\mathbf x},-\widehat{\mathbf y})$
        \STATE \textbf{if} $\checkrestart(r;\mathcal H)$ \textbf{then break} \COMMENT{Adaptive restart}
        \STATE $\mathbf z\gets\dfrac{\mathbf z^{\mathrm a}+(k+1)(2\widehat{\mathbf z}-\mathbf z)}{k+2}$\COMMENT{Reflected Halpern}
        \STATE $k\gets k+1$
    \ENDLOOP

    \STATE $j\gets j+1$
    \STATE$w\gets\updateweight(\widehat{\mathbf z},\mathbf z^{\mathrm a},w;j)$\COMMENT{Adaptive weight}
    \STATE $\mathbf z^{\mathrm a}\gets\widehat{\mathbf z}$
\ENDLOOP
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Active-Support Initialization and Updates}

At each finer level, we initialize the active support using dual potentials from a coarser UOT solution, then refine the active support while controlling its size.

\paragraph{Active-support initialization.}
For UOT problems sharing one side, changes in that side's normalized optimal marginal-response shape are bounded in terms of the cost interaction relative to its marginal penalty (Proposition~\ref{prop:uot-hierarchy-stability}, Appendix~\ref{app:uot-hierarchy-stability}).
This stability motivates a shared-side hierarchy following \hello~\citep{xia2026dual}, with adjacent levels changing only one side.
We inherit the shared-side potential, complete the other by a $c$-transform, and reconcile the potentially unequal marginal-response totals using the optimal translation of \citet[Proposition~2]{sejourne2022faster}:
\begin{equation*}
(\mathbf f,\mathbf g)\leftarrow(\mathbf f+t^\star\mathbf1_m,\mathbf g-t^\star\mathbf1_n).
\end{equation*}
Here $t^\star$ maximizes the dual objective over scalar shifts, while all dual scores remain unchanged (see Appendix~\ref{app:hierarchy-init}).

The resulting scores $\boldsymbol\sigma^0$ guide active-support initialization: writing $\operatorname{Top}_k(\boldsymbol\sigma)$ for the union of the top-$k$ edges in each row and column, we construct
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal N=\operatorname{Top}_{\kappa}(\boldsymbol\sigma^0)\cup\mathcal B.
\end{equation*}
Here $\mathcal B=\nw(\mathcal P)$ contains the structural edges constructed from the reference weights, which prevent the active support from imposing additional marginal mismatch under strong marginal penalties (see Appendix~\ref{app:structural-mismatch}).

\paragraph{Active-support updates.}
After each restricted solve, we expand the active support using positive-score candidates selected by the same bidirectional top-$k$ rule:
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal V=\operatorname{Top}_{\gamma}(\boldsymbol\sigma)\cap\{(i,j):\sigma_{ij}>0\}.
\end{equation*}
These candidates are identified by \pricing without storing the full cost matrix (see Appendix~\ref{app:hierarchy-pricing}).

The existence of a sparse optimum does not guarantee that an approximate restricted solution leaves sufficient room for new candidates within the support budget.
When needed, \sparsify mixes two cycle-cancellation outputs with costs bracketing the original cost, yielding at most $2(m+n-1)$ positive entries while preserving both transported marginals and the UOT objective.

Finally, \prune removes the lowest-score edges outside $\supp(\mathbf X)\cup\mathcal B\cup\mathcal V$ until the support budget is met (see Appendix~\ref{app:hierarchy-pruning}).
Together with tiled pricing, this ensures $O(m+n)$ memory for fixed feature dimension and support-budget parameters, as shown in Table~\ref{tab:support-size-bounds}.

\subsection{Convergence Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

We establish convergence of \subsolver and finite termination of \ours, accounting for their adaptive controls and stopping rules.


\begin{theorem}[Convergence of \subsolver, informal]
\label{prop:pduot-convergence}
\subsolver with the summable weight-update budget~\eqref{eq:pduot-weight-budget} satisfies:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Global convergence.}
For arbitrary mapped-point restart times, the iterates and mapped points converge to a restricted KKT point from any initial anchor.
\item \textbf{Local linear convergence.}
With sufficiently frequent residual-reduction checks and refreshed baselines after weight updates, convergence is locally R-linear in PDHG evaluations.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

The summable weight-update budget controls metric variation, while mapped-point restarts preserve a common descent estimate.
Residual-reduction checks further yield local linear convergence, as detailed in Appendices~\ref{app:pduot-global}--\ref{app:pduot-restart}.

With inexact restricted solves, positive dual scores need not imply comparable attainable primal improvement.
We therefore couple restricted stopping with objective decrease and pricing accuracy to establish finite refinement termination.
For refinement round $k$, write $\Delta_k=\Phi(\mathbf X^k)-\Phi(\mathbf X^{k+1})$ and $d_k=\varepsilon_{\mathrm r}^2(1+|\Phi(\mathbf X^k)|)$.
Let $G_k$ denote the maximum coordinate gain over newly added candidates, $v_k$ and $a_k$ the full-space and active-support positive-score maxima, and $\Omega_k$ the one-sided marginal-gradient error (defined in Appendix~\ref{app:refinement-convergence}).

\begin{theorem}[Finite refinement termination, informal]
\label{thm:refinement}
Suppose each restricted call returns in finite time to tolerance $\varepsilon_{\mathrm r}>0$, and consecutive refinement outputs satisfy
\begin{equation*}
\begin{gathered}
\Delta_k\geq G_k/2,\\
\Delta_k\geq d_k
\quad\text{or}\quad
\max\{a_{k+1},\Omega_{k+1}\}\leq v_k/2.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation*}
Then refinement terminates in finitely many rounds at restricted tolerance $\varepsilon_{\mathrm r}$ and any prescribed full-space tolerance $\tau>0$.
\end{theorem}

The \checkstop operator in \subsolver enforces these output conditions, which are attained in finitely many iterations under the restricted-solver assumptions (see Appendix~\ref{app:refinement-local-stopping}).
The proof combines objective decrease with violation contraction: rounds without sufficient decrease either contract the maximum dual violation or ensure a uniformly positive decrease in a subsequent round, as detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:refinement-convergence}.

\section{Extensions}
\label{sec:extensions}

We further extend \ours to entropic UOT using sparse active-support and to UFGW by embedding it within block coordinate descent.

\subsection{Sparse Entropic UOT}
\label{sec:entropic-extension}

We approximate entropic UOT with regularization strength $\varepsilon>0$ on a sparse active support $\mathcal N_{\mathrm e}$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sparse-entropic-extension}
\min_{X\geq0,\,\supp(X)\subseteq\mathcal N_{\mathrm e}}\;\Phi(\mathbf{X})+\varepsilon\KL(\mathbf{X}\,|\,\mathbf{a}\otimes \mathbf{b}).
\end{equation}
Using the final solution returned by \ours, we set
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal N_{\mathrm e}=\supp(\mathbf X)\cup\operatorname{Top}_{b_{\mathrm e}}(\boldsymbol\sigma),
\end{equation*}
where the budget $b_{\mathrm e}$ can be chosen automatically to fit the final active-support size (see Appendix~\ref{app:entropic-extension}).
Generalized Sinkhorn updates on this support require $O(|\mathcal N_{\mathrm e}|+m+n)=O(m+n)$ time and memory per iteration and return a sparse coupling for downstream tasks; we call this method \ourssparsink.

\subsection{Unbalanced Fused Gromov--Wasserstein}
\label{sec:ufgw-extension}

We consider UFGW with $\ell_2^2$ structural and feature costs, combining feature matching with alignment of within-dataset relations.
In its two-coupling relaxation~\citep{sejourne2023unbalanced}, fixing $\mathbf Q$ gives the KL-UOT subproblem
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\mathbf P\geq0}\,
\langle\mathbf C^{\mathbf Q},\mathbf P\rangle
+|\mathbf Q|(\rho_a\KL(\mathbf P\mathbf1\,|\,\mathbf a)
+\rho_b\KL(\mathbf P^\top\mathbf1\,|\,\mathbf b)),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
where $|\mathbf Q|=\sum_{kl}Q_{kl}$ and $\mathbf C^{\mathbf Q}$ is the cost induced by $\mathbf Q$ (see Appendix~\ref{app:ufgw-extension}).
Block coordinate descent thus produces a sequence of UOT subproblems, which we solve with \ours or, for the entropic variant, \ourssparsink.
We further evaluate costs implicitly from sparse coupling moments, keeping the overall algorithm's memory linear in the number of points for fixed feature dimensions.

\section{Conclusion}
\ours combines dual-guided edge localization with a GPU-parallel restricted KL-UOT solver to solve large transport problems under a linear storage budget.
The analysis connects shared-side response stability to hierarchical initialization and establishes convergence guarantees for the solver with adaptive restart and protected weight updates, and for safeguarded refinement under stated conditions.
Experiments evaluate unregularized accuracy, scaling, and component contributions, while sparse entropic refinement and UFGW extend the framework to biological alignment tasks.

Sparse entropic refinement depends on the localized support, and the UFGW extension optimizes a nonconvex objective.

\section{Hierarchy Operations}
\label{app:hierarchy-operations}

\paragraph{Hierarchy construction.}
The \build operation follows the nested random subsampling of \hello~\citep{xia2026dual}.
We independently permute the source and target nodes once, together with their reference weights, and form all coarser levels from prefixes of these fixed permutations.
At each step, the larger side is reduced from $N$ nodes to $\lceil N/s\rceil$ using a fixed split factor $s\geq2$, while the other side is unchanged; ties are resolved by reducing the source side.
Coarsening stops when both sides have at most the prescribed coarsest-size threshold, and the resulting problems are solved in reverse order, from level $L$ to level $0$.
Each level retains the selected reference weights: normalized-reference experiments rescale each side to unit total mass, whereas runs preserving absolute reference masses use the selected weights without rescaling.
The penalties $\rho_a,\rho_b$ and the ground-cost definition remain fixed across levels.

\subsection{Initialization and Potential Translation}
\label{app:hierarchy-init}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:initialize} specifies \init for adjacent levels that share one side.
The shared potential is inherited, and a c-transform completes the other side on the finer level.
For the resulting potentials, define the two response totals
\begin{equation*}
Z_a=\sum_i a_i e^{-f_i/\rho_a},\qquad Z_b=\sum_j b_j e^{-g_j/\rho_b}.
\end{equation*}
With the propagated potentials fixed, optimal translation means maximizing the dual objective over the scalar shift:
\begin{equation*}
t^\star=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{t\in\mathbb R}D(\mathbf f+t\mathbf1_m,\mathbf g-t\mathbf1_n).
\end{equation*}
Along this score-preserving direction, the dual objective has derivative $e^{-t/\rho_a}Z_a-e^{t/\rho_b}Z_b$ and strictly negative second derivative.
Its unique maximizer is~\citep[Proposition~2]{sejourne2022faster}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:potential-translation}
t^\star=\frac{\rho_a\rho_b}{\rho_a+\rho_b}(\log Z_a-\log Z_b).
\end{equation}
The translated marginal responses have equal total mass.
We apply this formula to the inter-level warm start; all dual scores and hence the dual-assigned edges are unchanged.
The logarithms of $Z_a$ and $Z_b$ are evaluated by log-sum-exp to avoid forming large exponentials.
At restricted-solver termination checks, we optionally apply the same score-preserving translation and accept the translated output only if all stopping conditions pass.
A rejected translation leaves the iteration state unchanged.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\init: finer-level initialization}
\label{alg:initialize}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} finer problem $\mathcal P$, adjacent coarse state $\mathcal Z^{\mathrm c}$, structural edges $\mathcal B$, and budget $\kappa$.
\STATE \textbf{Output:} active support $\mathcal N$ and initial state $\mathcal Z$.
\IF{the target side is shared}
    \STATE Inherit $\mathbf g\gets\mathbf g^{\mathrm c}$.
    \STATE Complete $f_i\gets\min_j(C_{ij}-g_j)$ for every source $i$ by a tiled scan.
\ELSE
    \STATE Inherit $\mathbf f\gets\mathbf f^{\mathrm c}$.
    \STATE Complete $g_j\gets\min_i(C_{ij}-f_i)$ for every target $j$ by a tiled scan.
\ENDIF
\STATE Compute $t^\star$ from~\eqref{eq:potential-translation}.
\STATE $\mathbf f\gets\mathbf f+t^\star\mathbf1_m$, $\mathbf g\gets\mathbf g-t^\star\mathbf1_n$.
\STATE For each source, retain its $\kappa$ largest-score edges; let their union be $\mathcal C_s$.
\STATE For each target, retain its $\kappa$ largest-score edges; let their union be $\mathcal C_t$.
\STATE $\mathcal N\gets\mathcal C_s\cup\mathcal C_t\cup\mathcal B$, with duplicate edges removed.
\STATE $\mathbf X\gets0$ on $\mathcal N$, and $\mathcal Z\gets(\mathbf X,\mathbf f,\mathbf g)$.
\STATE \textbf{Return:} $(\mathcal N,\mathcal Z)$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:initialize} gives the mathematical operations; the implementation reuses completion scans to collect dual-assignment candidates where possible.
When a side has fewer than $\kappa$ nodes, all available neighbors are retained.

\paragraph{Structural edges and marginal mismatch.}
\label{app:structural-mismatch}
For strictly positive reference masses, define the minimum marginal mismatch on an active support $\mathcal N$ by
\begin{equation*}
\Gamma_{\mathcal N}=\min_{\substack{\mathbf X\geq0\\\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf X)\subseteq\mathcal N}}\left\{\KL(\mathbf X\mathbf1_n\mid\mathbf a)+\KL(\mathbf X^\top\mathbf1_m\mid\mathbf b)\right\}.
\end{equation*}
The objective is continuous with bounded sublevel sets on this closed feasible set, so the minimum is attained.
Since KL divergence is nonnegative and vanishes only at equality, $\Gamma_{\mathcal N}=0$ if and only if $\mathcal N$ supports a coupling with marginals $\mathbf a,\mathbf b$.
Otherwise, every coupling supported on $\mathcal N$ incurs the strictly positive lower bound
\begin{equation*}
\rho_a\KL(\mathbf X\mathbf1_n\mid\mathbf a)+\rho_b\KL(\mathbf X^\top\mathbf1_m\mid\mathbf b)\geq\min\{\rho_a,\rho_b\}\Gamma_{\mathcal N}>0.
\end{equation*}
When the reference totals agree and $\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal N$, the northwest-corner coupling witnesses $\Gamma_{\mathcal N}=0$.
Protecting $\mathcal B$ during pruning preserves this property throughout refinement.
This guarantees that exact reference-marginal matching remains possible; the UOT optimum may still favor marginal deviations to reduce transport cost.

\subsection{Full-Space Pricing}
\label{app:hierarchy-pricing}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:pricing} computes the full-space certificate and bidirectional candidate edges in one tiled traversal.
The normalized violation residual is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hierarchy-full-violation}
\eta=\frac{\|[\boldsymbol\sigma]_+\|_p}{1+\|\mathbf C\|_p},\qquad \sigma_{ij}=f_i+g_j-C_{ij},\qquad p\in\{2,\infty\}.
\end{equation}
Here the matrix entries are treated as a vector, so $p=2$ denotes the Frobenius norm and $p=\infty$ the largest absolute entry.
For $p=2$, each tile contributes to the sums of squared positive scores and squared costs; for $p=\infty$, the accumulators retain the corresponding maxima.
Only tile costs, nodewise top-$\gamma$ buffers, and scalar norm accumulators are stored.
The norm policy agrees with the restricted solve: $p=2$ by default, with $p=\infty$ available at the finest level.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\pricing: streamed full-space pricing}
\label{alg:pricing}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} problem $\mathcal P$, state $\mathcal Z=(\mathbf X,\mathbf f,\mathbf g)$, and budget $\gamma$.
\STATE \textbf{Output:} candidate edges $\mathcal V$ and full-space dual-violation residual $\eta$.
\STATE Initialize source and target top-$\gamma$ buffers and scalar norm accumulators.
\FOR{each source--target tile $I\times J$}
    \STATE Evaluate $C_{ij}$ and $\sigma_{ij}=f_i+g_j-C_{ij}$ for $(i,j)\in I\times J$.
    \STATE Merge tile scores into the top-$\gamma$ buffers of all incident source and target nodes.
    \STATE Accumulate the norms of $[\boldsymbol\sigma]_+$ and $\mathbf C$ for~\eqref{eq:hierarchy-full-violation}.
\ENDFOR
\STATE $\mathcal V\gets$ the union of buffered edges with strictly positive scores, with duplicates removed.
\STATE Compute $\eta$ from the accumulated norms using~\eqref{eq:hierarchy-full-violation}.
\STATE \textbf{Return:} $(\mathcal V,\eta)$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Candidate edges already in $\mathcal N$ are ignored during insertion; the full-space certificate includes every pair regardless of support membership.

After a successful restricted solve, the hierarchy reserves space for structural edges and the next bidirectional pricing batch.
The positive-flow cap is $\lceil\beta(m+n)\rceil-(m+n-1)-\gamma(m+n)$.
For $\beta\geq\gamma+3$, this cap accommodates the union of two forest-supported couplings.
If the positive support exceeds the cap, two cycle-cancellation passes construct sparse couplings with the same transported marginals and transport costs bracketing the original cost.
A convex combination restores the original transport cost while retaining both marginals, so the KL-UOT primal objective is preserved up to floating-point arithmetic.
The dual potentials are retained, and restricted certificates are refreshed after sparsification.
Budgeted pruning then protects every positive-flow edge, the structural edges, and the newly inserted candidates, removing only zero-flow edges in decreasing order of dual slack until $|\mathcal N|\leq\lceil\beta(m+n)\rceil$.

\paragraph{Cost-preserving construction.}
Let $\mathbf X\geq0$ have transported marginals $\mathbf r,\mathbf s$ and cost $q=\langle\mathbf C,\mathbf X\rangle$.
Cycle cancellation on the bipartite support graph preserves both marginals, and each cycle admits an orientation that does not increase cost and an orientation that does not decrease it.
Applying the two orientations in separate passes gives $\mathbf X^-,\mathbf X^+$ with costs $q^-\leq q\leq q^+$.
If $q^-<q^+$, set
\begin{equation*}
\theta=\frac{q^+-q}{q^+-q^-},\qquad
\widehat{\mathbf X}=\theta\mathbf X^-+(1-\theta)\mathbf X^+.
\end{equation*}
Then $\widehat{\mathbf X}$ has marginals $\mathbf r,\mathbf s$ and transport cost $q$; if $q^-=q^+$, either output suffices.
Consequently, any objective consisting of transport cost and marginal penalties, including KL and squared-$\ell_2$ penalties, is unchanged.
In exact arithmetic, each pass can continue to a forest with at most $m+n-1$ edges, so a cost-preserving output with at most $2(m+n-1)$ edges always exists within the input support.
The implementation may stop earlier, once each pass fits half the reserved positive-flow cap, since the support of their mixture lies in the union of the two outputs.
This explains why the cap above suffices for $\beta\geq\gamma+3$.
Preserving the primal objective and retaining the potentials also preserves their primal--dual objective difference, although individual residuals and their support-dependent normalizations need not remain unchanged.
The production solver therefore refreshes the restricted diagnostics on the unchanged active graph before subsequent budgeted pruning.

\paragraph{Empirical scaling.}
We evaluate the two-pass construction on cyclic sparse supports with $m=n$ ranging from $2^{10}$ to $2^{20}$, using four ground costs and three feature dimensions.
All 360 recorded runs satisfy the prescribed output-support cap.
The maximum absolute source and target marginal errors are below $1.1\times10^{-18}$, and the maximum absolute objective error is below $1.1\times10^{-11}$ for both KL and squared-$\ell_2$ penalties.
These controlled tests exercise compression directly; they do not estimate how often sparsification triggers on real hierarchy trajectories.

\section{\subsolver Analysis and Control Steps}

\paragraph{Restricted formulation.}
For columnwise vectorization of $\mathbf X$, the full marginal-sum matrix is
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf A=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf1_n^\top\otimes\mathbf I_m\\\mathbf I_n\otimes\mathbf1_m^\top\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb R^{(m+n)\times mn}.
\end{equation*}
The restricted matrix $\mathbf A_{\mathcal N}$ retains the columns indexed by $\mathcal N$.
\begin{definition}[Restricted UOT]
\label{def:restricted-uot}
Vectorizing the active entries of~\eqref{eq:restricted-uot} gives~\eqref{eq:restricted-uot-vector}.
Its dual is
\begin{equation*}
\max_{\mathbf f,\mathbf g}D(\mathbf f,\mathbf g)\quad\text{s.t. }f_i+g_j\leq C_{ij},\ (i,j)\in\mathcal N.
\end{equation*}
The objective is unchanged from~\eqref{eq:dual-uot}; only the active inequalities are retained.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{KL proximal evaluation.}
We use $\operatorname{prox}_{G^*}^{\mathbf D_y^{-1}}(\mathbf u)=\arg\min_{\mathbf v}\{G^*(\mathbf v)+\tfrac12\|\mathbf v-\mathbf u\|_{\mathbf D_y^{-1}}^2\}$.
For dual input $u$, reference mass $h>0$, penalty $\rho>0$, and step $\mathbf{s}>0$, the coordinate proximal problem is
\begin{equation*}
\min_v\;\rho h(e^{v/\rho}-1)+\frac{(v-u)^2}{2\mathbf{s}}.
\end{equation*}
Its optimality equation $v-u+\mathbf{s} h e^{v/\rho}=0$ has the unique solution $v=u-\rho W((\mathbf{s} h/\rho)e^{u/\rho})$, where $W$ is the principal Lambert--$W$ function.
To avoid forming the exponential, let $\ell=\log(\mathbf{s} h/\rho)+u/\rho$ and solve $e^q+q=\ell$ for $q$.
Then $e^q=W(e^\ell)$ and $v=u-\rho e^q$; equivalently, $v=\rho(q-\log(\mathbf{s} h/\rho))$.
This log-domain formulation supports scalar evaluation without explicitly constructing $e^{u/\rho}$.

\subsection{PDHG Maps and Adaptive Global Convergence}
\label{app:pduot-global}

We first establish the fixed-metric facts used within each epoch, then control metric changes across adaptive restarts.
Throughout this appendix, fix $\mathcal N$ and abbreviate $\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal N}$.
All proximal operations in the proofs are exact.
Within a restart epoch, Algorithm~\ref{alg:pduot} applies reflected Halpern iteration:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:halpern}
\mathbf z^{k+1}=\frac{1}{k+2}\mathbf z^{\mathrm a}+\frac{k+1}{k+2}(2\pduotmap\mathbf z^k-\mathbf z^k).
\end{equation}
Here $\mathbf z^{\mathrm a}$ is the fixed epoch anchor and $\mathbf z^0=\mathbf z^{\mathrm a}$.
For a positive definite matrix $\mathbf{P}$, write $\|\mathbf{z}\|_\mathbf{P}=(\mathbf{z}^\top \mathbf{P}\mathbf{z})^{1/2}$ and $\operatorname{dist}_\mathbf{P}(\mathbf{z},S)=\inf_{u\in S}\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{u}\|_\mathbf{P}$.
Define the restricted KKT mapping
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pduot-kkt-map}
\mathcal M(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{c}+\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal N}^\top \mathbf{y}+N_{\mathbb R_+^{|\mathcal N|}}(\mathbf{x})\\ \nabla G^*(\mathbf{y})-\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal N}\mathbf{x}\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $N_{\mathbb R_+^{|\mathcal N|}}$ is the convex normal-cone mapping.
The zeros of $\mathcal M$ are precisely the restricted KKT points, with $\mathbf{y}=-(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})$.
The mapping $\mathcal M$ is maximally monotone: $\partial F\times\partial G^*$ is maximally monotone, and the remaining linear map $(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\mapsto(\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal N}^\top \mathbf{y},-\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal N}\mathbf{x})$ is skew-symmetric and defined everywhere.

For fixed positive diagonal steps, define the PDHG metric
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf{P}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{D}_x^{-1}&-\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal N}^\top\\-\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal N}&\mathbf{D}_y^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation*}

\begin{proof}[Fixed-metric facts within an epoch]
The restricted primal objective is continuous on the nonnegative orthant and coercive: both transported marginal totals equal $\|\mathbf x\|_1$, so the KL penalties grow superlinearly in $\|\mathbf x\|_1$, whereas the finite transport costs contribute a linear term.
It therefore attains a minimum $\mathbf x^\star$.
Node coverage makes its transported marginals strictly positive, as shown in Appendix~\ref{app:pduot-sharpness}.
Setting $\mathbf y^\star=\nabla G(\mathbf A_{\mathcal N}\mathbf x^\star)$, the primal first-order optimality condition and conjugacy give $0\in\mathcal M(\mathbf x^\star,\mathbf y^\star)$, so the restricted KKT solution set is nonempty.
The step-size condition makes $\mathbf P$ positive definite by the Schur complement.
For $\widehat{\mathbf{z}}=T\mathbf{z}$, the two proximal optimality conditions in~\eqref{eq:pduot-map} are
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf{D}_x^{-1}(\mathbf{x}-\widehat{\mathbf{x}})-\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal N}^\top \mathbf{y}\in\partial F(\widehat{\mathbf{x}}),\qquad
\mathbf{D}_y^{-1}(\mathbf{y}-\widehat{\mathbf{y}})+\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal N}(2\widehat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x})=\nabla G^*(\widehat{\mathbf{y}}).
\end{equation*}
Rearranging gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pduot-resolvent}
\mathbf{P}(\mathbf{z}-T\mathbf{z})\in\mathcal M(T\mathbf{z}),\qquad T=(\mathbf{P}+\mathcal M)^{-1}\mathbf{P}.
\end{equation}
Monotonicity applied to the images of two points $\mathbf{z},\mathbf{u}$ yields
\begin{equation*}
\|T\mathbf{z}-T\mathbf{u}\|_\mathbf{P}^2\leq\langle T\mathbf{z}-T\mathbf{u},\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{u}\rangle_\mathbf{P}.
\end{equation*}
Thus $T$ is firmly nonexpansive, $R:=2T-\mathbf{I}$ is nonexpansive, and $\operatorname{Fix}R=\operatorname{Fix}T=\mathcal M^{-1}(0)$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:halpern}, initialized at $\mathbf{z}^0=\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm a}$, is the Halpern iteration for $R$ with coefficients $1/(k+2)$.
The Halpern convergence theorem~\citep{wittmann1992approximation} and the residual estimate of \citet[Theorem~2.1]{lieder2021convergence}, applied to $R$ in the $\mathbf P$ inner product, give convergence to a fixed point and
\begin{equation*}
\|\mathbf{z}^k-R\mathbf{z}^k\|_\mathbf{P}\leq\frac{2\operatorname{dist}_\mathbf{P}(\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm a},\operatorname{Fix}R)}{k+1}.
\end{equation*}
Since $\mathbf{z}^k-R\mathbf{z}^k=2(\mathbf{z}^k-T\mathbf{z}^k)$, we obtain
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pduot-halpern-bound}
\|\mathbf z^k-T\mathbf z^k\|_{\mathbf P}\leq\frac{\operatorname{dist}_{\mathbf P}(\mathbf z^{\mathrm a},\operatorname{Fix}T)}{k+1}.
\end{equation}
Continuity of $T$ also gives convergence of the mapped points to the same KKT point.
\end{proof}

The degree-based steps in~\eqref{eq:pduot-metric} satisfy the required norm condition without estimating a spectral norm.
Indeed, Cauchy--Schwarz at each node gives, for any edge vector $\mathbf{h}$,
\begin{equation*}
\|\overline{\mathbf{D}}_y^{1/2}\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal N}\overline{\mathbf{D}}_x^{1/2}\mathbf{h}\|_2^2
=\frac{\zeta^2}{2}\sum_v\frac{1}{d_v}\left(\sum_{e\ni v}\mathbf{h}_e\right)^2
\leq\frac{\zeta^2}{2}\sum_v\sum_{e\ni v}\mathbf{h}_e^2
=\zeta^2\|\mathbf{h}\|_2^2.
\end{equation*}
Each edge has two endpoints, and the reciprocal factors $w$ in $\mathbf{D}_x$ and $\mathbf{D}_y$ cancel in the scaled incidence matrix.
Hence its norm is at most $\zeta<1$ for every fixed $w>0$.

\subsection{Support-Induced Iteration Lower Bound}
\label{app:hall-iteration-bound}

The following result connects a support restriction to the number of PDHG-map evaluations needed to pass the marginal-consistency test.
It applies to fixed steps and to restarts that reset the anchor without changing the map.

\begin{proposition}[Iteration lower bound from a weighted Hall deficit]
\label{prop:hall-iteration-lower-bound}
Fix an active support $\mathcal N$, positive reference masses $\mathbf a,\mathbf b$, finite costs, and $\rho_a=\rho_b=\rho$.
Suppose a source set $S$ and its active neighbors $J=\{j:(i,j)\in\mathcal N\text{ for some }i\in S\}$ satisfy
\begin{equation*}
A_S:=\sum_{i\in S}a_i>B_J:=\sum_{j\in J}b_j>0.
\end{equation*}
The same result holds with the source and target sides exchanged.
Choose fixed positive diagonal steps satisfying $\|\mathbf D_y^{1/2}\mathbf A_{\mathcal N}\mathbf D_x^{1/2}\|_2<1$, and let $T_\rho$ be~\eqref{eq:pduot-map} with metric $\mathbf P$ defined above.
Starting at $\mathbf z^0=(\mathbf x^0,-\mathbf f^0,-\mathbf g^0)$, use either ordinary PDHG or reflected Halpern~\eqref{eq:halpern}, optionally restarting at any iterate or mapped point computed in the current epoch.
Each new epoch starts at its new anchor, with the same map and coefficients.
Write $d_\rho=\|T_\rho\mathbf z^0-\mathbf z^0\|_{\mathbf P}>0$ and $M_0=\max\{\|\mathbf f^0\|_\infty,\|\mathbf g^0\|_\infty\}$.
For a fixed $\delta\geq0$, define
\begin{equation*}
\eta_\delta=\sqrt{|S|+|J|}\,\delta<A_S-B_J,\qquad \ell_\delta=\log\frac{2A_S}{\sqrt{\eta_\delta^2+4A_SB_J}+\eta_\delta}>0.
\end{equation*}
Any mapped output $(\mathbf x,\mathbf f,\mathbf g)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hall-marginal-target}
\left\|\mathbf A_{\mathcal N}\mathbf x-\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf a\odot e^{-\mathbf f/\rho}\\\mathbf b\odot e^{-\mathbf g/\rho}\end{pmatrix}\right\|_2\leq\delta
\end{equation}
requires at least
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hall-iteration-lower-bound}
N\geq\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf P)}}{d_\rho}\,[\rho\ell_\delta-M_0]_+
\end{equation}
evaluations of $T_\rho$.
Consequently, for fixed support, data, steps, and threshold, initial states uniformly bounded as $\rho\to\infty$ require $N=\Omega(\rho)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathbf r,\mathbf q$ be the output marginals and $\widehat{\mathbf r}=\mathbf a\odot e^{-\mathbf f/\rho}$, $\widehat{\mathbf q}=\mathbf b\odot e^{-\mathbf g/\rho}$ their responses.
The mapped coupling is nonnegative, so the support restriction gives $\sum_{i\in S}r_i\leq\sum_{j\in J}q_j$.
Writing $M=\max\{\|\mathbf f\|_\infty,\|\mathbf g\|_\infty\}$, Cauchy--Schwarz and~\eqref{eq:hall-marginal-target} imply
\begin{equation*}
A_Se^{-M/\rho}-B_Je^{M/\rho}\leq\sum_{i\in S}\widehat r_i-\sum_{j\in J}\widehat q_j\leq\eta_\delta.
\end{equation*}
Solving this quadratic inequality in $e^{M/\rho}$ gives $M\geq\rho\ell_\delta$.
Hence every qualifying output satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hall-required-travel}
\|\mathbf z-\mathbf z^0\|_{\mathbf P}\geq\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf P)}\,[\rho\ell_\delta-M_0]_+.
\end{equation}

By~\eqref{eq:pduot-resolvent}, $T_\rho$ is firmly nonexpansive in the $\mathbf P$ norm.
For ordinary PDHG, successive displacements are therefore at most $d_\rho$, giving $\|\mathbf z-\mathbf z^0\|_{\mathbf P}\leq Nd_\rho$ after $N$ evaluations.
Restarting at an already computed iterate has the same bound, since its map displacement cannot exceed that of the epoch anchor.
To obtain the same bound for reflected Halpern and its restarts, set $R=2T_\rho-\mathbf I$ and consider an epoch with anchor $\mathbf u$, iterates $\mathbf h^0=\mathbf u$, and $e=\|T_\rho\mathbf u-\mathbf u\|_{\mathbf P}$.
Nonexpansiveness of $R$ and the coefficients in~\eqref{eq:halpern} give, by induction,
\begin{equation*}
\|\mathbf h^k-\mathbf u\|_{\mathbf P}\leq ke,\qquad \|\mathbf h^{k+1}-\mathbf h^k\|_{\mathbf P}\leq e.
\end{equation*}
Indeed, the first bound follows from $D_{k+1}\leq\frac{k+1}{k+2}(D_k+2e)$ with $D_k=\|\mathbf h^k-\mathbf u\|_{\mathbf P}$.
For the second, $\Delta_1\leq e$ and comparison of consecutive updates yields
\begin{equation*}
\Delta_{k+1}\leq\frac{k+1}{k+2}\Delta_k+\frac{\|R\mathbf h^{k-1}-\mathbf u\|_{\mathbf P}}{(k+1)(k+2)}\leq\frac{k+1}{k+2}e+\frac{e}{k+2}=e.
\end{equation*}
The identity $(k+1)(R\mathbf h^k-\mathbf h^k)=(k+2)(\mathbf h^{k+1}-\mathbf h^k)+(\mathbf h^k-\mathbf u)$ then gives $\|T_\rho\mathbf h^k-\mathbf h^k\|_{\mathbf P}\leq e$.
Thus the mapped output from the epoch's $(k+1)$st evaluation satisfies
\begin{equation*}
\|T_\rho\mathbf h^k-\mathbf u\|_{\mathbf P}\leq(k+1)e,\qquad \|T_\rho(T_\rho\mathbf h^k)-T_\rho\mathbf h^k\|_{\mathbf P}\leq e.
\end{equation*}
Restarting at either an iterate or a mapped point therefore cannot increase the anchor's initial map displacement.
Summing the displacement bounds over epochs gives $\|\mathbf z-\mathbf z^0\|_{\mathbf P}\leq Nd_\rho$, regardless of the restart times.
Combining this bound with~\eqref{eq:hall-required-travel} proves~\eqref{eq:hall-iteration-lower-bound}.

Finally, bounded initial states yield uniformly bounded first primal updates and dual proximal inputs for fixed steps and costs.
For $\rho\geq\rho_{\min}>0$, the scalar equation $v-u+s h e^{v/\rho}=0$ implies $u-s h e^{\max\{u,0\}/\rho_{\min}}\leq v\leq u$.
Thus $d_\rho$ is uniformly bounded, proving the stated asymptotic lower bound.
\end{proof}

For the relative $\ell_2$ marginal criterion in Appendix~\ref{app:stopping-criterion}, take $\delta=(1+\|(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)\|_2)\varepsilon$; the threshold condition requires this test to resolve the Hall deficit.
The result concerns a fixed support and metric: it does not cover adaptive primal-weight updates or initial potentials that already grow with $\rho$.
When the reference totals agree, retaining $\mathcal B$ excludes a weighted Hall deficit and removes this particular obstruction, without implying a penalty-independent convergence rate.

\subsection{Positive Marginals and Local Sharpness}
\label{app:pduot-sharpness}

We first justify positivity of the transported marginals under the node-coverage assumption in Proposition~\ref{prop:pduot-convergence}.
Let $\mathbf{x}^\star$ be a restricted optimum and suppose its source marginal $r_i^\star$ is zero.
Choose an incident active edge $e=(i,j)$ and add flow $t>0$ on that edge.
The source KL change divided by $t$ is $\rho_a(\log(t/a_i)-1)$, which tends to $-\infty$ as $t\downarrow0$.
The cost quotient is finite, while the target KL quotient tends either to the finite value $\rho_b\log(q_j^\star/b_j)$ if $q_j^\star>0$, or to $-\infty$ otherwise.
The total quotient is therefore negative for sufficiently small $t$, contradicting optimality.
The target argument is symmetric, so $\mathbf{r}^\star,\mathbf{q}^\star>0$.

\paragraph{KKT error bound.}
Introduce the transported marginal vector $\mathbf m=(\mathbf r,\mathbf q)$ and write the restricted problem as
\begin{equation*}
\min_{\mathbf m,\mathbf x}\;G(\mathbf m)+F(\mathbf x)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\mathbf A_{\mathcal N}\mathbf x-\mathbf m=0.
\end{equation*}
This problem satisfies the structured polyhedricity assumption of \citet[Assumptions~1.2--1.3]{yuan2020discerning}.
Indeed, the extended-valued KL function $G$ is proper, closed, and convex, is differentiable on the positive orthant, and has
\begin{equation*}
\nabla^2G(\mathbf m)=\operatorname{diag}(\rho_a/\mathbf r,\rho_b/\mathbf q)\succ0.
\end{equation*}
Thus $G$ is strongly convex on every compact convex subset of its subdifferential domain, its gradient is locally Lipschitz on the positive orthant, and the gradient norm diverges when approaching a finite boundary point.
In the notation of that assumption, take the smooth representation matrix to be the identity and the additional linear term to be zero.
The function $F$ is convex piecewise linear-quadratic, since it is a linear function plus the indicator of the nonnegative orthant.
Consequently, \citet[Theorem~59]{yuan2020discerning} gives metric subregularity of the lifted KKT mapping at every lifted KKT point, without rank or strict-complementarity assumptions.

To transfer this bound to~\eqref{eq:pduot-kkt-map}, define, for $\mathbf m>0$,
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal K(\mathbf m,\mathbf x,\mathbf y)=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla G(\mathbf m)-\mathbf y\\ \mathbf c+\mathbf A_{\mathcal N}^{\top}\mathbf y+N_{\mathbb R_+^{|\mathcal N|}}(\mathbf x)\\ \mathbf m-\mathbf A_{\mathcal N}\mathbf x\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
Up to signs and ordering of the residual blocks, this is the KKT mapping in the cited theorem.
Write $\mathcal S=\mathcal M^{-1}(0)$ and $\widetilde{\mathcal S}=\mathcal K^{-1}(0)$, and fix $\mathbf z^\star=(\mathbf x^\star,\mathbf y^\star)\in\mathcal S$.
The projection of $\widetilde{\mathcal S}$ onto $(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)$ is exactly $\mathcal S$.
For $\mathbf z=(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)$ near $\mathbf z^\star$, set $\mathbf m(\mathbf y)=\nabla G^*(\mathbf y)$.
This positive vector depends continuously on $\mathbf y$, and $\nabla G(\mathbf m(\mathbf y))=\mathbf y$.
The lifted error bound therefore yields, for some $\kappa>0$ and a neighborhood $\mathbf U$ of $\mathbf z^\star$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pduot-kkt-error-bound}
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{dist}_2(\mathbf z,\mathcal S)&\leq\operatorname{dist}_2((\mathbf m(\mathbf y),\mathbf x,\mathbf y),\widetilde{\mathcal S})\\
&\leq\kappa\operatorname{dist}_2(0,\mathcal K(\mathbf m(\mathbf y),\mathbf x,\mathbf y))\\
&=\kappa\operatorname{dist}_2(0,\mathcal M(\mathbf z)),\qquad \mathbf z\in\mathbf U.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This bound measures distance to the entire solution set and allows nonunique optimal couplings, cycles in the positive-flow support, and edges with both zero flow and zero slack.

\paragraph{Fixed-point sharpness.}
Let $p_{\max}$ be the largest eigenvalue of $\mathbf P$.
For $\mathbf z$ sufficiently near $\mathbf z^\star$, continuity gives $T\mathbf z\in\mathbf U$.
Combining~\eqref{eq:pduot-kkt-error-bound} with~\eqref{eq:pduot-resolvent} and $\|\mathbf P\mathbf h\|_2\leq\sqrt{p_{\max}}\|\mathbf h\|_{\mathbf P}$ gives
\begin{equation*}
\operatorname{dist}_{\mathbf P}(T\mathbf z,\mathcal S)\leq\sqrt{p_{\max}}\kappa\|\mathbf P(\mathbf z-T\mathbf z)\|_2\leq\kappa p_{\max}\|\mathbf z-T\mathbf z\|_{\mathbf P}.
\end{equation*}
The triangle inequality then yields the local sharpness estimate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pduot-local-sharpness}
\alpha\operatorname{dist}_{\mathbf P}(\mathbf z,\mathcal S)\leq\|\mathbf z-T\mathbf z\|_{\mathbf P},\qquad \alpha=(1+\kappa p_{\max})^{-1}>0.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Practical Control Steps}
\label{app:pduot-controls}
\paragraph{Preconditioning.}
We set $\mathbf D_x=w\overline{\mathbf D}_x$ and $\mathbf D_y=\overline{\mathbf D}_y/w$ using Pock--Chambolle base steps.
For node-covering active supports with node degrees $d_v$, these are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pduot-metric}
(\overline{\mathbf D}_x)_{ee}=\frac{\zeta}{2},\qquad (\overline{\mathbf D}_y)_{vv}=\frac{\zeta}{d_v},\qquad 0<\zeta<1.
\end{equation}
They satisfy $\|\mathbf D_y^{1/2}\mathbf A_{\mathcal N}\mathbf D_x^{1/2}\|_2<1$ for every $w>0$; the base steps remain fixed within a restricted solve.

\paragraph{Restart and primal-weight updates.}
The scalar $r$ returned by \pdhg is the fixed-point residual $\|\mathbf z-\widehat{\mathbf z}\|_{\mathbf P}$ in the current PDHG metric.
The restart history $\mathcal H$ stores the epoch baseline, the previous checked residual, iteration counters, and first-check status; it is initialized empty at the start of each restricted solve.
The predicate $\checkrestart(r;\mathcal H)$ returns a Boolean and updates this history internally.
After a restart, it refreshes the epoch baseline at the next call under the new metric while retaining the cumulative iteration count and first-check status.
\checkrestart tests fixed-point residual reduction relative to the epoch anchor, deterioration relative to the previous check after moderate reduction, and an epoch-length threshold.
The practical reduction factors are $0.2$ and $0.5$, and the epoch-length threshold is $0.36$ times the total iteration count; the first restart check also starts a new epoch.
At a restart, let $d_x=\|\widehat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm a}\|_{\overline{\mathbf{D}}_x^{-1}}$ and $d_y=\|\widehat{\mathbf{y}}-\mathbf{y}^{\mathrm a}\|_{\overline{\mathbf{D}}_y^{-1}}$.
For valid, nonnegligible displacements, \updateweight forms $e=\log(d_x/d_y)-\log w$, updates an integral state $I\leftarrow\nu I+e$, and sets
\begin{equation*}
w^+ = \operatorname{clip}\bigl(\exp(\log w+k_pe+k_iI),w_{\min},w_{\max}\bigr).
\end{equation*}
When these displacement or residual checks are invalid, the implementation falls back to a previously retained weight with better primal--dual residual balance and resets the integral state.
With $L=\log(w_{\max}/w_{\min})$, the $j$th weight update satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pduot-weight-budget}
\left|\log\frac{w_j}{w_{j-1}}\right|\leq b_j:=L\min\{1,(J/j)^p\},\qquad p>1.
\end{equation}
The update counter resets at each restricted solve, so the first $J$ updates are unrestricted by this additional budget.
Every proposal, including fallback, then passes through~\eqref{eq:pduot-weight-budget}: its log displacement from the current weight is clipped to $[-b_j,b_j]$.
If a newly retained weight is clipped, the retained value is replaced by the accepted weight; the integral update is otherwise unchanged.
We use $w_{\min}=10^{-12}$, $w_{\max}=10^{12}$, $J=64$, and $p=1.01$.
Appendix~\ref{app:pduot-weight-budget-experiment} examines actual clipping with smaller $J$.
Changing the metric starts a new epoch; the implementation refreshes the mapped point and residual baseline under that metric.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:pduot} presents the control structure with one outer iteration per restart epoch.
For clarity, its stopping and restart tests are shown at each iteration; the implementation evaluates them periodically, reusing the last residual between checks.

\section{Stopping Criterion}
\label{app:stopping-criterion}

At the mapped point, \checkstop evaluates marginal consistency $\delta_{\mathrm m}$, stationarity $\delta_{\mathrm s}$, and relative gap $\delta_{\mathrm g}$, requiring
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pduot-stopping}
\max\{\delta_{\mathrm m},\delta_{\mathrm s},\delta_{\mathrm g}\}\leq\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
The refinement safeguard checks, their scope of application, and their finite attainability under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{prop:pduot-convergence} are specified in Appendix~\ref{app:refinement-local-stopping}.
This section defines these measures, then analyzes the gap and its balanced-OT limit.
All stopping measures are evaluated in the original problem coordinates on a fixed active support $\mathcal N$.

\subsection{Residuals and Termination}

For a PDHG input $\mathbf{z}=(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})$ and its mapped point $\widehat{\mathbf{z}}=(\widehat{\mathbf{x}},\widehat{\mathbf{y}})=T\mathbf{z}$, write $(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{q})=\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal N}\widehat{\mathbf{x}}$ and $(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})=-\widehat{\mathbf{y}}$.
The marginal responses are $\widehat{\mathbf{r}}=\mathbf{a}\odot e^{-\mathbf{f}/\rho_a}$ and $\widehat{\mathbf{q}}=\mathbf{b}\odot e^{-\mathbf{g}/\rho_b}$, and the restricted dual slack is $\mathbf{s}_{\mathcal N}=\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal N}-\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal N}^\top(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})$.
We define the marginal-consistency and stationarity residuals by
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\delta_{\mathrm m}&=\frac{\|(\mathbf{r}-\widehat{\mathbf{r}},\mathbf{q}-\widehat{\mathbf{q}})\|}{1+\|(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\|},\\
\delta_{\mathrm s}&=\frac{\|\mathbf{s}_{\mathcal N}-\boldsymbol{\lambda}\|}{1+\|\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal N}\|},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
where $\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\mathbf{D}_x^{-1}(\widehat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x})+\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal N}+\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal N}^\top \mathbf{y}$ is the bound multiplier supplied by the primal projection.
The projection ensures $\widehat{\mathbf{x}}\geq0$, $\boldsymbol{\lambda}\geq0$, and $\lambda_e\widehat x_e=0$.
Consequently, zero stationarity residual implies restricted dual feasibility and complementarity, while zero marginal-consistency residual gives the KL marginal optimality relations.
Together these conditions imply the restricted KKT conditions.

Let $t=\langle \mathbf{c}_{\mathcal N},\widehat{\mathbf{x}}\rangle$ and $d=\langle \mathbf{f},\widehat{\mathbf{r}}\rangle+\langle \mathbf{g},\widehat{\mathbf{q}}\rangle$.
The relative gap is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pduot-split-gap}
\delta_{\mathrm g}=\frac{|t-d|}{1+|t|+|d|}.
\end{equation}
\checkstop accepts the mapped point only when~\eqref{eq:pduot-stopping} and all applicable refinement safeguard checks pass.
The optimal-anchor readout in Appendix~\ref{app:refinement-local-stopping} provides an additional return path subject to the same restricted tests and its stated safeguard checks.
The residual norms are $\ell_2$ by default, with an $\ell_\infty$ option at the finest hierarchy level.
These tests concern the restricted problem; Algorithm~\ref{alg:ours} separately checks dual violations outside the active support through full-space pricing.
Its outer stopping test $\operatorname{KKT}(\mathcal P,\mathcal Z)$ combines the accepted restricted-solve certificate and the pricing residual as $\max\{\delta_{\mathrm m},\delta_{\mathrm s},\delta_{\mathrm g},\eta\}$, reusing the latest solver and pricing results without an additional full-space scan.

\subsection{Gap Criterion and the Balanced Limit}
\label{app:gap-criterion}

For the following analysis, $\mathbf{x}$ denotes a generic nonnegative evaluation point, including the mapped coupling used above.
Let $\Phi_{\mathcal N}(\mathbf{x})$ be the restricted primal objective, and let $D(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})$ be the dual objective in~\eqref{eq:dual-uot} evaluated at the current potentials.
Write $(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{q})=\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal N}\mathbf{x}$, $\widehat{\mathbf{r}}=\mathbf{a}\odot e^{-\mathbf{f}/\rho_a}$, and $\widehat{\mathbf{q}}=\mathbf{b}\odot e^{-\mathbf{g}/\rho_b}$.

\paragraph{Original objective gap.}
The generalized-KL identity
\begin{equation*}
\rho_a\KL(\mathbf{r}\,|\,\mathbf{a})+\rho_a\sum_i a_i(e^{-f_i/\rho_a}-1)
=-\langle \mathbf{f},\mathbf{r}\rangle+\rho_a\KL(\mathbf{r}\,|\,\widehat{\mathbf{r}})
\end{equation*}
and its target counterpart give the exact decomposition
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:original-gap-decomposition}
\Phi_{\mathcal N}(\mathbf{x})-D(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})
=\langle \mathbf{c}_{\mathcal N},\mathbf{x}\rangle-\langle \mathbf{f},\mathbf{r}\rangle-\langle \mathbf{g},\mathbf{q}\rangle
+\rho_a\KL(\mathbf{r}\,|\,\widehat{\mathbf{r}})+\rho_b\KL(\mathbf{q}\,|\,\widehat{\mathbf{q}}).
\end{equation}
When the potentials are dual feasible on $\mathcal N$, the first three terms form a nonnegative complementarity term, and the entire expression is a primal--dual gap bounding restricted objective suboptimality.
At arbitrary infeasible dual iterates, it remains an objective difference without this bound.
The divergence terms weight marginal-response errors by the penalty coefficients.
For positive $\widehat{\mathbf{r}}$ and $\mathbf{r}$ near $\widehat{\mathbf{r}}$, for example,
\begin{equation*}
\rho_a\KL(\mathbf{r}\,|\,\widehat{\mathbf{r}})
=\frac{\rho_a}{2}\sum_i\frac{(r_i-\widehat r_i)^2}{\widehat r_i}
+o(\|\mathbf{r}-\widehat{\mathbf{r}}\|_2^2),
\end{equation*}
where the expansion holds for fixed $\rho_a$ and $\widehat{\mathbf{r}}$.
Thus a prescribed marginal residual tolerance does not give a penalty-independent bound on the original objective gap.

\paragraph{Gap for the split optimality system.}
Introducing independent transported marginals yields the equivalent split formulation
\begin{equation*}
\min_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{r},\mathbf{q}\geq0}\;\langle \mathbf{c}_{\mathcal N},\mathbf{x}\rangle+\rho_a\KL(\mathbf{r}\,|\,\mathbf{a})+\rho_b\KL(\mathbf{q}\,|\,\mathbf{b})
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \mathbf{A}_{\mathcal N}\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{q}).
\end{equation*}
Its marginal responses are the minimizers of the marginal Lagrangian terms for given $(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})$.
At these responses, $\nabla(\rho_a\KL)(\widehat{\mathbf{r}}\,|\,\mathbf{a})=-\mathbf{f}$ and $\nabla(\rho_b\KL)(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}\,|\,\mathbf{b})=-\mathbf{g}$.
Pairing the stationarity relations with primal variables motivates the signed consistency quantity
\begin{equation*}
H=\langle \mathbf{c}_{\mathcal N},\mathbf{x}\rangle-\langle \mathbf{f},\widehat{\mathbf{r}}\rangle-\langle \mathbf{g},\widehat{\mathbf{q}}\rangle,
\qquad
\delta_{\mathrm g}=\frac{|H|}{1+|\langle \mathbf{c}_{\mathcal N},\mathbf{x}\rangle|+|\langle \mathbf{f},\widehat{\mathbf{r}}\rangle+\langle \mathbf{g},\widehat{\mathbf{q}}\rangle|}.
\end{equation*}
This is the gap in~\eqref{eq:pduot-split-gap}, expressed at a generic evaluation point.
Its exact relation to the original objective difference is
\begin{equation*}
\Phi_{\mathcal N}(\mathbf{x})-D(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})
=H-\langle \mathbf{f},\mathbf{r}-\widehat{\mathbf{r}}\rangle-\langle \mathbf{g},\mathbf{q}-\widehat{\mathbf{q}}\rangle
+\rho_a\KL(\mathbf{r}\,|\,\widehat{\mathbf{r}})+\rho_b\KL(\mathbf{q}\,|\,\widehat{\mathbf{q}}).
\end{equation*}
At exact marginal consistency, $H$ equals the original objective difference.
At finite tolerance, $\delta_{\mathrm g}$ measures consistency of the split system and is combined with $\delta_{\mathrm m}$ and $\delta_{\mathrm s}$.
In particular, exact marginal consistency and stationarity with a nonnegative complementary bound multiplier imply the restricted KKT conditions and global restricted optimality.
The scalar $\delta_{\mathrm g}$ alone does not bound original-objective suboptimality.

\paragraph{Compatibility with balanced OT.}
Consider equal-total-mass references and a support admitting a balanced transport plan.
For fixed bounded potentials, as $\rho_a,\rho_b\to\infty$ the marginal responses approach $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$, respectively.
Consequently, the gap expression tends to
\begin{equation*}
\frac{|\langle \mathbf{c}_{\mathcal N},\mathbf{x}\rangle-\langle \mathbf{a},\mathbf{f}\rangle-\langle \mathbf{b},\mathbf{g}\rangle|}
{1+|\langle \mathbf{c}_{\mathcal N},\mathbf{x}\rangle|+|\langle \mathbf{a},\mathbf{f}\rangle+\langle \mathbf{b},\mathbf{g}\rangle|},
\end{equation*}
and the marginal-response residual becomes the balanced marginal-feasibility residual.
These are the corresponding balanced-OT stopping quantities.
The original dual objective also has the balanced limit at bounded potentials; the distinction is the nonuniform penalty weighting of marginal errors in~\eqref{eq:original-gap-decomposition} along approximately feasible primal iterates.

For a concrete example, take one edge with $a=b=1$, $c=f=g=0$, $x=1+\delta>0$, and equal penalties $\rho$.
Then $\widehat r=\widehat q=1$, $\delta_{\mathrm g}=\delta_{\mathrm s}=0$, and $\delta_{\mathrm m}=\sqrt{2}|\delta|/(1+\sqrt{2})$ under the $\ell_2$ policy.
Meanwhile,
\begin{equation*}
\Phi_{\mathcal N}(x)-D(f,g)=2\rho\bigl((1+\delta)\log(1+\delta)-\delta\bigr),
\end{equation*}
which is asymptotic to $\rho\delta^2$ for small $\delta$.
For any fixed nonzero $\delta$, the usual relative original-objective gap tends to one as $\rho\to\infty$, even though the marginal residual remains at a fixed small scale.
Thus imposing a small original-objective gap can require increasingly accurate marginals as the penalties grow.
\subsolver instead applies a common tolerance to the three split optimality measures, preserving the balanced-OT stopping scale; original-objective gaps are evaluated separately when required for numerical comparisons.

\section{Sparse Entropic Refinement}
\label{app:entropic-extension}

On a fixed support $\mathcal{N}$, define $K_{ij}=a_i b_j\exp(-C_{ij}/\varepsilon)$ for $(i,j)\in\mathcal{N}$ and zero otherwise.
The supported coupling has the form $X_{ij}=u_iK_{ij}v_j$, with generalized scaling updates
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf{u}\leftarrow\left(\frac{\mathbf{a}}{\mathbf{K}\mathbf{v}}\right)^{\rho_a/(\rho_a+\varepsilon)},\qquad
\mathbf{v}\leftarrow\left(\frac{\mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{K}^\top \mathbf{u}}\right)^{\rho_b/(\rho_b+\varepsilon)}.
\end{equation*}
Our implementation evaluates the scaling iterations in the log domain using sparse row and column reductions on the GPU.
The KL term in~\eqref{eq:sparse-entropic-extension} uses the full reference $\mathbf{a}\otimes \mathbf{b}$, including its constant contribution outside $\mathcal{N}$, when evaluating the objective.

\paragraph{Support reconstruction.}
Let $\mathbf X^0$ be the unregularized solution, $\mathcal N_\star$ its final active support, and $\boldsymbol\sigma$ its final dual scores.
For a per-direction edge budget $b_{\mathrm e}\geq1$, reconstruct the support as
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal N_{\mathrm e}=\supp(\mathbf{X}^0)\cup\operatorname{RowTop}_{b_{\mathrm e}}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\cup\operatorname{ColTop}_{b_{\mathrm e}}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}).
\end{equation*}
The top-score selections include nonpositive scores and require an additional full-space scan.
Writing $s=|\mathcal N_\star|$ and $s_+=|\supp(\mathbf X^0)|$, automatic reconstruction selects
\begin{equation*}
b_{\mathrm e}=\min\left\{32,\left\lfloor\frac{s-s_+}{m+n}\right\rfloor\right\}.
\end{equation*}
For $b_{\mathrm e}\geq1$, the bound $|\mathcal N_{\mathrm e}|\leq s_++b_{\mathrm e}(m+n)\leq s$ preserves the final active-support budget; $32$ is the current scan implementation's top-$k$ limit.
If the automatic budget is zero, we reuse $\mathcal N_\star$.
Direct reuse, $\mathcal N_{\mathrm e}=\mathcal N_\star$, is also available as a separate variant that avoids the reconstruction scan.

\section{Two-Coupling UFGW Updates}
\label{app:ufgw-extension}

\paragraph{Objective.}
Let $\mathbf A\in\mathbb R^{m\times m}$ and $\mathbf B\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}$ encode within-dataset relations, and let $\mathbf M\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}$ be the feature cost.
We use a two-coupling relaxation with tensorized KL marginal penalties~\citep{sejourne2023unbalanced}.
% CN: 请作者补入原始 UGW relaxation 论文的引用；此处暂用现有综述引用，不修改 bibliography。
% EN: Add the original UGW relaxation citation; the existing survey is cited provisionally without changing the bibliography.
For nonnegative couplings $\mathbf P,\mathbf Q$, define
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal Q_{A,B}(\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)=\sum_{i,k,j,l}(A_{ik}-B_{jl})^2P_{ij}Q_{kl}
\end{equation*}
and $D_a(\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)=\KL((\mathbf P\mathbf1)\otimes(\mathbf Q\mathbf1)\,|\,\mathbf a\otimes\mathbf a)$, with $D_b$ defined analogously for target marginals.
The relaxed objective is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ufgw-relaxation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{P,Q\geq0}\quad &(1-\alpha)\mathcal Q_{A,B}(\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)\\
&+\frac{\alpha}{2}\langle\mathbf M,\mathbf P+\mathbf Q\rangle+\rho_aD_a(\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)+\rho_bD_b(\mathbf P,\mathbf Q),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha\in[0,1]$ controls feature matching and $\alpha=0$ gives the UGW relaxation.

\paragraph{Reduction to KL-UOT.}
Write $|\mathbf{Q}|=\sum_{kl}Q_{kl}$ and $\mathbf{q}_a=\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{1}$, $\mathbf{q}_b=\mathbf{Q}^\top\mathbf{1}$.
The tensorized marginal term satisfies
\begin{equation*}
\KL(\mathbf{p}\otimes \mathbf{q}\,|\,\mathbf{a}\otimes \mathbf{a})
=|\mathbf{q}|\KL(\mathbf{p}\,|\,\mathbf{a})+|\mathbf{p}|\sum_kq_k\log(q_k/a_k)+|\mathbf{a}|(|\mathbf{a}|-|\mathbf{q}|).
\end{equation*}
Consequently, fixing $\mathbf{Q}$ in~\eqref{eq:ufgw-relaxation} yields, up to constants independent of $\mathbf{P}$,
\begin{equation*}
\min_{P\geq0}\;\langle \mathbf{C}^\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{P}\rangle+\rho_a|\mathbf{Q}|\KL(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{1}\,|\,\mathbf{a})+\rho_b|\mathbf{Q}|\KL(\mathbf{P}^\top\mathbf{1}\,|\,\mathbf{b}),
\end{equation*}
where
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{C}^Q_{ij}&=(1-\alpha)\sum_{kl}(A_{ik}-B_{jl})^2Q_{kl}+\frac{\alpha}{2}M_{ij}+\kappa_Q,\\
\kappa_Q&=\rho_a\sum_k(\mathbf{q}_a)_k\log((\mathbf{q}_a)_k/a_k)+\rho_b\sum_l(\mathbf{q}_b)_l\log((\mathbf{q}_b)_l/b_l).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The $\mathbf{Q}$ block is obtained symmetrically; our structural matrices are symmetric squared-Euclidean distances.
The prototype initializes with the product coupling $\mathbf{a}\otimes \mathbf{b}$, stored implicitly, and alternates the two block solves.
After solving the $\mathbf{P}$ block, it applies $\mathbf{P}\leftarrow\sqrt{|\mathbf{Q}|/|\mathbf{P}|}\,\mathbf{P}$; after solving the $\mathbf{Q}$ block, it applies the symmetric rescaling using the current $\mathbf{P}$.
These steps match the block-coordinate implementation used in the experiments.
The outer procedure optimizes a nonconvex two-coupling relaxation; the restricted-solver convergence results do not imply global optimality of this relaxation or equality of the two returned couplings.

\paragraph{Entropic blocks.}
For normalized reference masses, the optional entropic objective adds $\varepsilon\KL(\mathbf{P}\otimes \mathbf{Q}\,|\,(\mathbf{a}\otimes \mathbf{b})\otimes(\mathbf{a}\otimes \mathbf{b}))$ to~\eqref{eq:ufgw-relaxation}.
With $\mathbf{Q}$ fixed, the block therefore acquires $\varepsilon|\mathbf{Q}|\KL(\mathbf{P}\,|\,\mathbf{a}\otimes \mathbf{b})$ and the scalar cost contribution $\varepsilon\sum_{kl}Q_{kl}\log(Q_{kl}/(a_kb_l))$.
We solve its unregularized counterpart with \ours and apply sparse entropic refinement on the resulting support.
The scalar cost translation can be absorbed into rescaled KL reference masses, retaining the same block minimizer without modifying the squared-distance solver.

\paragraph{Implicit structural costs.}
For squared-Euclidean structural matrices, write exact factorizations $\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{U}_A\mathbf{V}_A^\top$ and $\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{U}_B\mathbf{V}_B^\top$.
The linearized structural cost is
\begin{equation*}
L_Q=(\mathbf{A}\odot \mathbf{A})\mathbf{q}_a\mathbf{1}_n^\top+\mathbf{1}_m((\mathbf{B}\odot \mathbf{B})\mathbf{q}_b)^\top-2\mathbf{A}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{B}^\top.
\end{equation*}
Its cross term depends on the small moment matrix $\mathbf{V}_A^\top \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{V}_B$, and the two squared-distance terms can likewise be evaluated through small weighted moments.
More explicitly, the matrix
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf{H}_Q=\begin{pmatrix}
\mathbf{V}_A^\top\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{q}_a)\mathbf{V}_A&\mathbf{V}_A^\top \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{V}_B\\
\mathbf{V}_B^\top \mathbf{Q}^\top \mathbf{V}_A&\mathbf{V}_B^\top\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{q}_b)\mathbf{V}_B
\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}
is positive semidefinite because it is a sum of outer products weighted by $Q_{kl}$.
Factoring $\mathbf{H}_Q=\mathbf{F}\mathbf{F}^\top$ and setting $z_i=(\mathbf{U}_{A,i},0)\mathbf{F}$, $\mathbf{w}_j=(0,\mathbf{U}_{B,j})\mathbf{F}$ gives $(L_Q)_{ij}=\|z_i-\mathbf{w}_j\|_2^2$.
Scaling these embeddings by $\sqrt{1-\alpha}$ and concatenating feature vectors scaled by $\sqrt{\alpha/2}$ represents the nonconstant block cost exactly.
For fixed structural and feature dimensions, the embedding storage grows linearly with the number of points and the moment accumulation depends on the maintained coupling support.
